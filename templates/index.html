💡 Engineering Interpretation

  • Inlet Velocity (u_in, ~68.7%): Dominates heat extraction. Increasing fluid velocity thins thermal boundary layers and maximizes local convective heat transfer.
  • Nanoparticle Fraction (phi, ~29.2%): Secondary primary lever. Dispersed nanoparticles elevate thermal conductivity, accelerating conductive heat uptake from micro-channel walls.
  • Hartmann Number (Ha, ~2.1%): Fine-tuning influence. External magnetic fields dampen vortex shedding, maintaining stable laminar micro-channel flow.

🔬 Project Overview & Thermal Architecture

This system implements the machine learning thermal prediction component of KJS-CES-02: AI-Enabled Micro-Channel Battery Thermal Management System for Electric Vehicles.

⚠️ Operating Band Classifications

Temperature Risk Band Action
< 35.0 °C Safe Optimal cell performance
35.0 – 45.0 °C Moderate Increase pump circulation
45.0 – 55.0 °C High Initiate thermal throttling
≥ 55.0 °C Critical Thermal runaway risk; cutoff

📚 Dataset & Methodology

  • Dataset Source: Hugging Face (Dijo-404/mhd-nanofluid-ev-thermal-dataset).
  • Sample Size: 5,000 rows × 15 physical variables.
  • Algorithm: Scikit-learn RandomForestRegressor(n_estimators=200, random_state=42).
  • No Scaling Required: Decision tree ensembles naturally handle differing physical scales without standard scaling.

⚠️ Limitations & Future Scope

  • Simulation-Derived Data: Dataset represents numerical multiphysics simulations, not physical on-road vehicle sensors.
  • Sensor Noise & Aging: Battery cell impedance changes over thousands of charge cycles and experimental noise are not modeled.
  • Future Extensions: Integration with CAN-bus real-time BMS streams, dynamic PWM coolant pump controllers, and 3D spatial hotspot detection.

⚡ KJS-CES-02: Trained on simulation-derived data; not validated on real EV data.
